## LLMLingua-2
Mechanism
- Binary token classification (preserve/discard) over the full bidirectional context with a Transformer encoder, not self-information scoring [L2-001, L2-009].
- At inference it keeps the top-scoring words up to a budget and keeps original order, so output is hard text made only of original words [L2-002]; word score = mean of subword scores [L2-011]; ratio 1/tau is counted in words [L2-010].
- Two compressors: xlm-roberta-large (355M params, ~23 h training) = "LLMLingua-2", and multilingual-BERT (110M, ~16 h) = "LLMLingua-2-small"; 10 epochs, lr 1e-5, batch 10; training GPU not stated [L2-003, L2-004, L2-005, L2-006, L2-007].
- Training labels: GPT-4-32k compressions of MeetingBank transcripts, split into chunks of at most 512 tokens (a distillation-time chunk limit, not stated as an inference limit); 5,169 transcripts, 41,746 chunks, average 3,635 -> 1,415 tokens (2.57x) [L2-008, L2-013, L2-014, L2-014b, L2-012].
I/O
- Text in, text out; no model internals of the target LLM are used (inferred from mechanism) [L2-002, L2-082]. Paper contrasts itself with KV-cache methods that cannot be applied to black-box LLMs [L2-020].
- Target LLMs: GPT-3.5-Turbo (default, greedy, temp 0) and Mistral-7B; the same compressor was used for both [L2-021, L2-022, L2-023].
- Task-agnostic: compress once, reuse for any query; the paper motivates this with RAG re-compression cost [L2-009, L2-024].
Compression ratio and context loss (GPT-3.5-Turbo unless stated; question-agnostic)
- MeetingBank in-domain, ~3.1x: QA EM 86.92 vs 87.75 original; Rouge2 22.96 vs 26.66; BLEU 17.37 vs 22.34; BERTScore 88.27 vs 88.96 [L2-040, L2-042]. Small encoder: EM 85.82 [L2-041]. GPT-4 used directly as the compressor scores EM 84.86 at 2.5x [L2-056]. MeetingBank references (QA and summaries) are generated by GPT-4-32K [L2-119, L2-120].
- LongBench at 5x (2,000-token budget; per-task columns SingleDoc, MultiDoc, Summ., FewShot, Synth., Code): 29.8, 33.1, 25.3, 66.4, 21.3, 58.9, AVG 39.1; original 39.7, 38.7, 26.5, 67.0, 37.8, 54.2, AVG 44.0; zero-shot AVG 23.5 [L2-034, L2-043, L2-044]. At 3x: AVG 42.4 [L2-035]. ZeroSCROLLS AVG 33.4 (5x) and 33.5 (3x) vs 34.7 original [L2-034, L2-035, L2-043].
- GSM8K: 79.08 (5x) and 77.79 (14x) vs full-shot 78.85 and zero-shot 48.75. BBH: 70.02 (3x) and 61.94 (5x) vs full-shot 70.07 and zero-shot 32.32 [L2-032, L2-033, L2-049, L2-050].
- NaturalQuestions 20 docs, 4x, question-agnostic: 48.6/44.5/43.6/40.9/39.9 at the five answer positions vs original 75.7/57.3/54.1/55.4/63.1 and a single zero-shot value 56.1; the question-aware hybrid LLMLingua-2+ gets 74.0/70.4/67.0/66.9/65.3 [L2-059, L2-060, L2-061, L2-062, L2-063].
- LongBench-Zh at 5x: AVG 38.1 vs 42.5 original; Synth. 72.6 vs 77.5; SingleDoc 46.7 vs 61.2 [L2-057, L2-058].
- Mistral-7B target: MeetingBank QA 76.22 vs 66.95 original; LongBench SingleDoc 26.8 vs 24.5; authors speculate the original prompts exceed what Mistral handles well; on the under-8K subset ratios are only about 1.6x-2.6x [L2-051, L2-052, L2-053, L2-054, L2-055].
- Authors: small model is comparable to or slightly above original in some cases, but falls short of question-aware LongLLMLingua on LongBench [L2-047, L2-048]. Fixed per-sample ratio hurts; a dynamic per-sample ratio helps [L2-037, L2-038, L2-039]. Fig. 15 (1x-10x) is a figure only [L2-036].
Latency and compute
- V100-32G; Table 5 MeetingBank (s): end-to-end 14.9 uncompressed vs 9.4/7.5/5.2 with LLMLingua-2 at 2x/3x/5x (1.6x/2.1x/2.9x); compressor rows LLMLingua 2.9/2.1/1.5 vs LLMLingua-2 0.5/0.4/0.4 [L2-070, L2-071, L2-072, L2-078, L2-073]. Derived: LLMLingua / LLMLingua-2 = 5.8x, 5.3x, 3.8x, consistent with the abstract's 3x-6x [L2-077]; Selective-Context is about 32x-39x slower (derived from SC-T07).
- Peak GPU memory on MeetingBank: 2.1GB vs 16.6GB (LLMLingua) and 26.5GB (Selective-Context), described as 8x lower [L2-074, L2-075, L2-076].
Cost
- Tokens per request: MeetingBank 970 vs 3,003; LongBench 1,954 vs 10,295 [L2-080, L2-081, L2-042, L2-043]. No extra LLM call at inference (inferred); GPT-4 is used offline only [L2-082, L2-083].
Limitations and failure analysis
- Authors: training data is MeetingBank only; adding 50k TriviaQA-wiki examples helped but "not that significant" [L2-090, L2-091, L2-064].
- English-only training; only Chinese (5 LongBench tasks) tested outside English [L2-092, L2-093].
- Failure figures (13, 14) show OTHER datasets' (SentComp, DebateSum) compressions missing question-relevant text; no LLMLingua-2 failure case is shown [L2-094]. The paper's reconstruction showcase (Fig. 7) contains reconstructions beyond the compressed text (e.g. "20 out of 28 counties", a CNN sentence), which I flag as an observation, not the authors' claim [L2-095, L2-096, L2-097, L2-098]. Authors cite the reconstruction as evidence of no essential loss [L2-065].
Reproducibility
- Code link, released dataset, GitHub repo MIT, HF checkpoint card license mit (card heading mismatch noted) [L2-100, L2-101, L2-102, L2-103, L2-104].
Tasks tested
- Summarization (MeetingBank, LongBench Summ.), long-doc QA/RAG (LongBench, ZeroSCROLLS, NQ-20), reasoning (GSM8K, BBH) [L2-110, L2-113, L2-116, L2-118]. Coding: only a LongBench "Code" column, undescribed [L2-112]. Multi-turn chat, structured data, sensitive/PII: not tested in paper [L2-114, L2-115, L2-117, L2-111].

## Selective Context
Mechanism
- Self-information of each token from a base causal LM, merged into lexical units (token, phrase, sentence), then low-information units are dropped via a percentile threshold [SC-001, SC-002, SC-007].
- Base LM: LLaMA-7B for LLaMA/Vicuna targets; GPT-3 curie (via API) for OpenAI targets [SC-003, SC-004]. Self-information is computed sentence by sentence [SC-005]. Phrase units are spaCy noun chunks only; sentences use NLTK [SC-006]. Phrase level is best, token second, sentence unstable; numbers are only in a figure [SC-009, SC-056, SC-057]. Output is ungrammatical hard text [SC-008].
I/O
- Text in, text out; compressed text was given to GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 (API-only models) and to LLaMA-7/13/30B and Vicuna-7/13B [SC-010, SC-011]. A base-LM forward pass is required [SC-070].
Compression ratio
- Reduction ratios 0.2, 0.35, 0.5, 0.65, 0.8 = fraction of lexical units removed; a p=50 phrase-level example kept 57.2% of tokens [SC-020, SC-021, SC-022].
- Test items are under 2048 tokens, mean 690-865 tokens per document [SC-024, SC-025, SC-026, SC-083].
Context loss (agreement with the full-context LLM answer; no gold labels; averaged over target models) 
- Table 1 (temp 0.7): original row BERTScore-F1 .909; ratio 0.2 -> .902; 0.35 -> .897; 0.5 -> .887; 0.65 -> .877; 0.8 -> .863; BLEU .347 -> .295/.243/.179/.127/.070 [SC-030 to SC-035].
- Table 2 (greedy, vs random deletion): BERTScore-F1 at 0.2: SC .931 vs random .899; at 0.5: .900 vs .873; at 0.8: .866 vs .847 (the gap narrows at high ratio) [SC-036 to SC-041]. Table 2 and Table 1 use different protocols and are not comparable [SC-039, SC-033].
- Reference answers are LLM outputs, not human labels [SC-042, SC-043].
- Faithfulness (gpt-3.5-turbo, 200 QA per ratio, manual): unfaithful-tuple rate .027/.050/.038/.051/.086 at 0.2/0.35/0.5/0.65/0.8; refusals 0/6/4/19/27 [SC-045 to SC-050]. At 0.65, 19 refusals (9% of 200), answers 35% shorter [SC-051, SC-052].
- Task view: reconstruction drops steepest; summarisation and QA decline gradually, authors call it especially suitable for those (figure only) [SC-053, SC-054]. Instruct-tuned Vicuna is more robust than LLaMA, which often derails [SC-055]. Headline: BERTScore -.023 and faithfulness -.038 at the 50% point [SC-059]. Text says "0.32" BERTScore-F1 drop at 0.65/0.8, but Table 1 shows .032/.047 (likely typo) [SC-044, SC-044b].
Latency and compute
- One case study only (vicuna-13b, one arXiv paragraph, GPU type not stated): 77,695 MB and 110.8 ms/token uncompressed vs 61,885 MB and 76.3 ms/token at ratio 0.5; 46.1 ms to build the selective context [SC-060, SC-061, SC-062]. Headline claims 36% memory and 32% time reduction and "1.32 times faster per token" [SC-063, SC-064]. Derived from the Fig. 6 numbers: memory -20.3%, time -31.1% (1.45x per token); the paper does not explain the 36%.
- Third-party run in the LLMLingua-2 paper (LLaMA-2-7B SLM, V100-32G): 26.5GB peak memory and 15.9/15.6/15.5 s at 2x/3x/5x on MeetingBank, above the 14.9 s uncompressed end-to-end figure [SC-T07, SC-T08, SC-T09, L2-070]. Different workload from SC-062; do not compare directly.
- Third-party quality (same run, GPT-3.5-Turbo): MeetingBank EM 66.28 at 2.5x vs 87.75 original; LongBench AVG 24.8 (5x) / 32.0 (3x) vs 44.0, Code 49.2/53.1 vs 54.2; GSM8K 53.98 (5x) vs zero-shot 48.75 and full-shot 78.85; NQ-20 19.5-43.8 vs zero-shot 56.1 [SC-T01 to SC-T05, L2-042, L2-043, L2-049, L2-050, L2-061].
Limitations
- Authors: depends on noun-phrase boundary detection, no verb phrases, speculated dependency-tree filtering; best percentile varies by task and has no selection tool [SC-080, SC-081, SC-082].
- Evaluation limits: three custom test sets, contexts under 2048 tokens, agreement-only metrics, no per-language or per-scenario (code) breakdown [SC-083, SC-084, SC-085, SC-042].
- Qualitative failure: summary dropped research background pruned as redundancy [SC-058].
Reproducibility
- Code and data on GitHub; README says MIT; example uses gpt2 as base model [SC-090, SC-091, SC-092].
Tasks tested
- Summarisation and QA (arXiv, BBC), conversation (ShareGPT history -> last query), original-context reconstruction [SC-100, SC-106, SC-107]. Coding, structured data, math/reasoning, PII: not tested in paper [SC-101 to SC-105].

## Article vs paper disagreements (ledger ids)
- LLMLingua-2: ART-001 (wrong arXiv id/PDF), ART-002 (README authors/date), ART-003 (3x-6x faster), ART-004 (up to 20x with minimal loss), ART-005 (90%+ at moderate compression), ART-006 (teacher models for training), ART-007 (code prompts use case), ART-008 (model-specific encoders), ART-009 (chat use case), ART-010 (lost in the middle), ART-011 (more robust than Gist/500x), ART-012 (generation length/API cost).
- Selective Context: ART-013 (wrong paper), ART-014 (README authors/date), ART-015 (noun phrase boundaries), ART-016 (lower compression ratios than trained methods), ART-017 (parameter-free/lightweight/standalone), ART-018 (spaCy syntactic parsing preserves grammar).
- Net verdicts: 3x-6x speed is supported for the compression step (not end-to-end); 20x, 90%+, code use case, chat use case, lost-in-the-middle, and model-specific-encoder claims have no support in the paper; the GPT-4 teacher claim is true only for building training data; the noun-phrase "limit" is a partial reading of the authors' limitation.
